Korea, Japan seek closer customs cooperation on drugs, counterfeits

Summary

Korea and Japan are seeking to tighten customs cooperation on drug smuggling, counterfeit products and cross-border online shopping, as officials look to protect businesses and strengthen border security. Korea Customs Service Commissioner Lee Jong-wook met with Mitsuhiro Teraoka, director-general of Japan's Customs and Tariff Bureau, in Tokyo last week to discuss three areas of cooperation, the customs agency said Monday. The meeting followed the 34th Korea-Japan Customs Chiefs' Meeting in Seoul in April and was arranged separately to address pressing bilateral issues following Lee's appointment. One priority is protecting Korean and Japanese brands, including K-beauty products, from counterfeit goods. Korea proposed closer enforcement cooperation between the two countries' customs authorities and asked Japan to help Korean companies use its system for requesting customs authorities to block imports and exports of goods suspected of infringing intellectual property rights. The officials also discussed establishing a system to exchange intelligence on drug seizures and smuggling attempts,
