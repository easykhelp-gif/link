[2026 Updated] Foreigner's Guide to Korean Hospitals & Pharmacies

Using Hospitals in South Korea

South Korea has one of the best and most accessible healthcare systems in the world. However, if you are a foreign resident, navigating the medical system?îespecially the costs and emergency rooms?îcan be confusing.

Medical Costs: With vs. Without National Health Insurance (NHIS)

The cost of medical care in Korea depends entirely on whether you are enrolled in the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS). By law, any foreigner staying in Korea for more than 6 months must enroll in the NHIS.

If you are an undocumented immigrant or a short-term visitor, you will not have NHIS coverage, which means you must pay 100% out-of-pocket (Non-insured rate / ?ºÎ∞ò?òÍ?).

  • Emergency rooms in large hospitals are extremely expensive. If your condition is not life-threatening (e.g., a simple cut, stomachache), it is much cheaper and faster to visit a local clinic during normal hours.

Korean Pharmacy Rules (?ΩÍµ≠)

Unlike many countries, you cannot buy antibiotics, high-dose painkillers, or specialized medicines directly from a pharmacy without a doctor's prescription.
  • Over-The-Counter (OTC): You can buy basic painkillers (Tylenol), cold medicine, digestion pills, and bandages without a prescription.
  • Convenience Stores: At night when pharmacies are closed, you can buy emergency medicines (Tylenol, Panpyrin, Festal) at CU, GS25, or 7-Eleven.
